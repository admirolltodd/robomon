# Grafted Light

*The design philosophy behind ROBOMON's battle art. It was written with the canvas-design method and is applied by `tools/cyberize.py`.*

## The movement

Grafted Light treats every creature as a body that the city has worked on. Nothing in Neo-Kairo arrives whole. Animals are found in the alleys and then repaired, replaced or rewired, and the art records what was done to them. Each sprite should read as a document of that surgery. A viewer should be able to tell, at a glance and at sixty-four pixels, how much of the thing is still alive.

## Form: three degrees of replacement

There are three chassis classes, and they are three points on one line.

- **Robot.** The creature has been recast entirely in steel. Its silhouette survives, as if the city kept the animal's shape out of habit. The steel holds no colour of its own, only a cold ramp from gunmetal to chrome. Panel lines sit low on the body, never across the face, so the creature keeps an expression.
- **Cyborg.** Half the body is the animal and half is plate, and the join is the subject. That join is a single stepped seam, lit in neon and held shut with surgical staples. The plate always takes the rear of the body, the part turned away from the viewer, as if the work were done from behind.
- **Wetware.** The flesh stays, darker and slightly fevered. Circuitry runs under the skin in straight runs with right-angle turns, the way traces are routed on a board and the way no living thing grows. Steel ports break the surface where hardware was pushed in.

## Colour and light

Light comes from the street. Every creature carries a rim of its own type colour on the side that faces the city, plus a cold wash from above. Anything the original artist made bright and small, such as eyes, gems or markings, becomes a light source: a running light on a robot, an implant on a cyborg, bioluminescence on wetware. The glow lives in a layer of its own, so it can breathe and bloom when the creature is on screen. Nothing else glows. Restraint keeps the neon meaningful.

## Craft

This work is meant to look labored over, the product of deep expertise and painstaking attention. Every rule is placed to protect what the original artists drew:

- Seams stop short of faces.
- Veins stay two pixels inside the outline.
- Rivets sit only beside seams.
- Sprites are re-seated so every creature stands on the same floor line.

The pipeline is deterministic. The same creature always receives the same surgery, pixel for pixel, the way a master craftsman repeats a cut until it is exact.

## The plate

`LOG-bestiary.png` is the specimen sheet: all 43 Robomon, each shown front and back. It reads like the record of an imaginary discipline, a catalogue of grafts. It should be studied rather than glanced at.
